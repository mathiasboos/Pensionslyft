FIRE stands for "Financial Independence, Retire Early" and is both a financial strategy and a life philosophy. It is not only about money, but about time, our scarcest resource. The idea is simple: by saving aggressively and living frugally you build capital that lets you quit your job for good. You no longer trade time for money; you take back control of your time.

Ask yourself: would you go to work if you were not paid? If the answer is no, perhaps you should consider how you can shorten your working life and maximise the time for what really matters. The FIRE movement is often about exactly that: finding meaning, not just consumption. Family, friends, nature, volunteering, a passionate hobby or simply being: these are the things highlighted as real life goals.

Getting there does require sacrifices. It means sharply increasing your saving and cutting unnecessary spending. Minimalism and frugality are two core values of the movement. Simple lifestyle choices, few possessions and low consumption are keys to both lowering your spending and speeding up the way to financial independence.

The FIRE strategy is based on saving in shares and/or equity funds with the aim of building a large enough capital. With the help of the compound interest effect, the capital should be able to generate a passive income that covers your living costs for the rest of your life.

## Rule of thumb for the savings goal: 25 times your annual spending

If you want to live on your capital for about 30 years, you should aim to save at least 25 times your annual spending. But if you plan to retire as early as 40 and live another 50 years? Then it may be wiser to aim for 30 times your annual spending instead.

Think of it like this:

- Your monthly spending is 20,000 kronor.
- Your annual spending is then 20,000 kr x 12 = 240,000 kronor.
- Your savings goal is 240,000 kr x 25 = 6,000,000 kronor.

Your financial savings rate (saving in shares / income after tax) decides how quickly you reach the goal. If you save 50 % of your income it takes about 19 years to reach the goal; if you save 70 %, only 9 years.\* This assumes you invest the capital in, for example, a global equity fund with a real annual return of 5 % (return after inflation).

This is how many years it takes to reach the savings goal, depending on how much of your net salary you save:

| Savings rate | Number of years |
| --- | --- |
| 20 % | 51 |
| 30 % | 37 |
| 40 % | 26 |
| 50 % | 19 |
| 60 % | 14 |
| 70 % | 9 |
| 80 % | 5 |

Here is what the difference looks like. With traditional retirement, all income goes to spending throughout your working life, and after age 66 you live on the public pension, the occupational pension and withdrawals from your private savings:

With FIRE and a financial savings rate of 70 %, only 30 % of your net salary goes to spending. The rest becomes private savings until the capital is enough, and then you live on withdrawals from your savings. From age 67 the public pension and occupational pension are added:

## Rule of thumb for withdrawals: 4 % of the capital\*\*

Once you have reached the goal, the rule of thumb is that you can withdraw 4 % of your capital the first year and then adjust for inflation. The rule applies to a withdrawal over 30 years and to a portfolio with at least 50 % shares. For longer periods, for example if you want to live on the money for 50 years, it is wise to lower the withdrawal percentage, which also means the savings goal will probably be higher.

Several of those who reach financial freedom around 30–40 still choose not to stop working completely. They leave their regular jobs but go on to start their own companies or work part-time on something they are passionate about.

## FIRE and the pension system

The FIRE movement started in the USA in the 1990s and has spread to Sweden. There are books, blogs, forums, YouTubers and more, and I believe they have indirectly influenced how we view retirement age in Sweden. More and more people retire both before and after the guide age (riktålder) of the public pension (66 years, in 2025). There is no longer "one" fixed age at which we retire.

My thought is that if more people chose to check out at 40, it would affect the whole pension system. It is a question that has not yet received much attention, but that could become highly topical if the FIRE movement grows.

FIRE is not for everyone, but for those who are disciplined, think long-term and value freedom highly, it can give something priceless: control over your time.

And perhaps that is exactly the point. Not to stop working, but to stop having to.
