Bill Perkins's Die with Zero came out as early as 2020, but only now has it gained a foothold in Sweden. While the FIRE movement pushes for saving hard so you can stop working early, Perkins makes the case for living life to the fullest, starting now. His point? Experiences and memories are the real currency of life, and the earlier you create them, the longer you can enjoy them. It is like compound interest, but for joy in life.

Imagine backpacking in your 20s, living simply and collecting memories from adventures that may not feel as fun when you are 50 and prefer a comfortable hotel bed. Perkins argues that every experience has a "window", a time in life when it gives maximum return. If you miss the window, you miss something that will not come back. So why not spend money on a trip to Asia now, instead of saving everything for retirement?

But Die with Zero is not about spending all your money. It is a call to plan smartly. Perkins suggests dividing life into "time buckets", periods of life in which you match your goals with health, free time and money. When you are young you often have more time and health, but less cash. When you are older your wallet may be fatter, but the energy to climb mountains is gone. So plan for adventures early and save the cultural trips for later.

![Health declines with age while wealth grows. The activities you can afford, have time for and are healthy enough for are in the area where the curves meet, around age 60. (The text in the image is in Swedish.)](/artiklar/die-with-zero-halsa-och-formogenhet.png)

Another twist is to give money away while you are alive. Instead of waiting until the money automatically ends up with your heirs, usually after age 60, you can give support earlier, while the recipient actually has both the health and the energy to do something with it. According to Perkins, the optimal age for gifts is around 26–35, when the ability to think through consequences is in place but life is still full of possibilities. You also get to experience the joy of giving yourself, a bonus for a fulfilling life.

![Experience points from self-fulfilment over five years: life experiences are at roughly the same level every year, while the memories grow and stack on top. (The text in the image is in Swedish.)](/artiklar/die-with-zero-erfarenhetspoang.png)

But here is the catch: how do you know how much you need to save so you do not run out of money as a pensioner? Perkins's idea rests on assumptions about life expectancy and needs. In Sweden, every second 66-year-old woman is expected to live beyond 90, and for men it is 88.5.\* But nobody knows exactly how long you will live. That is why pensions with lifelong payments and safety nets have their point. If you plan wrong, the zero on your account can arrive before the end, and that is less appealing.

Surveys show that pensioners regret not having saved privately for retirement.\*\* Another experience that emerges is not to take out the occupational pension over too short a period. Since this year, many insurance companies let you pause your occupational pension or extend the payout period. This suggests that some pensioners do not have enough money and actually need to watch every krona.

So what is most exciting about Die with Zero? For me it is the idea of daring to live now, without sacrificing security later. Perkins's ideas are a refreshing contrast to the FIRE movement, but they are not without risks.

For me it is not either FIRE or "Die with Zero", it is a mix. A bit like the Swedish idea of *lagom*: save smartly, but live now.
