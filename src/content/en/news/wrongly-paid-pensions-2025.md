The Swedish Pensions Agency (Pensionsmyndigheten) paid out about 1.6 billion kronor wrongly in 2025. A little over 1 billion of that concerned housing supplement (bostadstillägg) and maintenance support for older people (äldreförsörjningsstöd). The report also points out four risk areas where incorrect information can lead to large payments.

Of the agency's total payments of 483 billion kronor, the wrongful payments made up 0.33 percent.

## Where the biggest risks are

The Pensions Agency estimates that these risk areas account for significant amounts of wrongful payments:

| Risk area | Estimated amount (million kr) |
| --- | ---: |
| Assets and debts reported incorrectly | 315 |
| Living or staying abroad | 312 |
| Income reported incorrectly | 253 |
| Pension from other countries | 249 |
| **Total** | **1,129** |

## Housing supplement and maintenance support dominate

A little over 1 billion kronor of the wrongful payments concerned housing supplement and maintenance support for older people. That is about two thirds of the total amount paid out wrongly.

According to the Pensions Agency, the errors usually happen because recipients miss, or do not know about, their duty to report changes in, for example, income, assets or place of residence. Deliberate fraud does occur, but it is not the most common explanation.

The agency therefore stresses how important it is that recipients give correct information, both when they apply and when their circumstances change.
