Pension savers who switch funds themselves are considerably more critical of the support for making fund choices than those who never switch. That is shown by a new evaluation from the Swedish Pensions Agency (Pensionsmyndigheten). Among the most active fund choosers, only about half think the decision support is enough.

For the first time, the Pensions Agency has evaluated the choice architecture of the premium pension system (premiepensionen). In the survey, savers and pensioners rated the premium pension guide (premiepensionsguiden), the fund marketplace (fondtorget) and the confirmation shown before a fund choice is made.

The results show clear differences between the groups. Of those who have made their own fund choice and then switched funds, **49.8 percent** think the information in the premium pension guide gives enough support. Among those who have made their own choice but never switched funds, the corresponding share is **70.4 percent**.

## The more involved, the more critical

The same pattern recurs in several parts of the survey. Those who have made many fund choices, and those who rate themselves as better informed, are more often critical of the decision support. The report's authors therefore conclude that the support may need to be adapted better to different groups of savers.

The fund switchers are most critical of the confirmation shown before a fund choice is carried out. Only about 40 percent think it gives enough support for making a well-considered fund choice. At the same time, nearly 90 percent of those who have switched funds themselves think the confirmation clearly explains that making your own fund choice means taking greater responsibility yourself.

## Few take the knowledge test

The evaluation also shows that relatively few use the knowledge test in the premium pension guide. Among those who made their own fund choices and visited the fund marketplace since the guide was introduced in 2024, about a quarter said they had taken the test.

The Pensions Agency also points out that the survey mainly measures how savers themselves experience the support. The next step should therefore be to study how the decision support actually affects the fund choices that are made.
