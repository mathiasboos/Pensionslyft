Sweden falls from sixth to eighth place in the 2026 Marsh CFA Institute Global Pension Index (formerly the Mercer Global Pension Index), even though the Swedish pension system's overall score improves. Australia and Chile overtake Sweden, while Sweden still ranks highly for long-term sustainability.

Sweden gets **78.8 points** in this year's global comparison, up from 78.2 last year. The grade remains B+. That means the system is judged to have a stable structure and many good features, but also areas for improvement that set it apart from the systems with the highest grade, A. The comparison covers 55 pension systems.

Sweden's loss of two places is therefore not due to a lower overall result. Australia and Chile improve more and so pass Sweden, which is still ahead of Finland and Norway.

| Country | 2025 | 2026 | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Australia | 77.6 | 80.6 | +3.0 |
| Chile | 76.6 | 79.2 | +2.6 |
| Sweden | 78.2 | 78.8 | +0.6 |

## Sweden's biggest improvement is in the adequacy of pensions

Sweden's biggest improvement is in the adequacy sub-index, which assesses how well the pension system provides financial security in retirement. The integrity sub-index, which covers regulation, governance and the protection of pension savers, among other things, also rises.

Sustainability is largely unchanged. Sweden still ranks sixth in this area, which takes into account pension assets, the coverage of pension systems and demographic conditions, among other things.

| Sweden's sub-indices | 2025 | 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adequacy | 76.8 | 78.2 |
| Sustainability | 76.3 | 76.2 |
| Integrity | 83.0 | 83.6 |

## Denmark climbs, the Netherlands keeps the lead

The Netherlands keeps first place with 85.8 points. Denmark overtakes Iceland and takes second place with 84.1 points, just a tenth of a point ahead of Iceland. Singapore, Israel and Australia follow. These six pension systems all get grade A.

According to the report, the strength of the Dutch system lies in the combination of a state pension with near-universal coverage, widely developed occupational pensions and voluntary private saving. Denmark gets the highest score for sustainability, while Finland tops the integrity sub-index.

## The pension index in brief

- **The top:** the Netherlands (85.8 points), Denmark (84.1) and Iceland (84.0).
- **Sweden:** eighth place with 78.8 points.
- **Best in the sub-indices:** Kuwait for adequacy, Denmark for sustainability and Finland for integrity.
- **How the result is calculated:** adequacy weighs 40 percent, sustainability 35 percent and integrity 25 percent.
- **Coverage:** 55 pension systems, with Kenya, Qatar and the Dominican Republic as this year's newcomers.
