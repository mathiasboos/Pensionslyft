A full working life of 40 years is no guarantee of a public pension equal to 60 percent of final salary. For an average earner, the public pension's share of final salary has fallen from 70 to 47 percent in just 20 years. At the same time, the occupational pension has become more and more important for pensioners' finances. This is shown by a new report from Folksam.

Folksam's report "Maxar ett långt arbetsliv pensionen?" ("Does a long working life max out the pension?") compares the pensions of people who retired in 2002 and in 2022. The result shows a clear change. An average earner with at least 40 years of work received in 2002 a public pension equal to 70 percent of final salary before tax. For the corresponding pensioner in 2022, the share had fallen to 47 percent.

For those with a shorter working life, the development has gone the same way. For them, the public pension fell from 63 to 44 percent of final salary over the period.

## The occupational pension has more than doubled

As the public pension has lost weight, the occupational pension (tjänstepension) has taken on a larger role. For an average earner with at least 40 years of work, the occupational pension more than doubled between 2002 and 2022. The payout rose from SEK 3,098 to SEK 6,666 a month before tax.

According to Folksam, half of the pensioners born in 1957 would have ended up below the relative poverty line if they had not had an occupational pension.

– For many, the occupational pension has become absolutely decisive for a secure economy in old age. The public pension is meant to offer financial security for everyone, including the roughly 500,000 people who have no occupational pension. Unfortunately, that is not the case today, says Håkan Svärdman, pension expert at Folksam.

## Only half reach 40 years of work

The report also shows that about half of the cohorts studied have worked and earned public pension rights for at least 40 years. It is mainly men with middle and high incomes who have had the longest working lives. Among people born in 1957, between 75 and 82 percent of the men in these income groups had worked for at least 40 years.

Folksam proposes several changes to strengthen the public pension and better reward a long working life. The proposals include a higher pension contribution, a modernised basic protection (grundskydd) and a more efficiently used "pensionsgas" (the pension "accelerator").

According to the report, a long working life does give a higher pension than a shorter one, but developments show that 40 years of work is no longer enough to secure a public pension at the level many expect.
