Unions and employers are negotiating new pension and insurance agreements for around two million employees. One of the big questions is why occupational pension contributions and insurance cover stop already at age 65, even though the guiding retirement age (riktålder) is now 67. If the parties do not agree, a strike could break out in December.

According to the newspaper Arbetet, the negotiations at Näringslivets hus in Stockholm are expected to continue all weekend. On one side is the Confederation of Swedish Enterprise (Svenskt Näringsliv), and on the other the unions within LO and PTK, which together represent blue-collar and white-collar workers in the private sector.

The talks concern changes to the collective agreements on occupational pensions and a number of insurances. A central issue is that the age limits for pension contributions and insurance cover need to be adapted to a longer working life.

LO's agreement council met on Friday morning to discuss the conditions ahead of the talks. On Monday, LO's board is to decide on the outcome.

– Our hope is that we agree as soon as possible, LO's bargaining secretary Veli-Pekka Säikkälä says in a text message to Arbetet.

PTK and Svenskt Näringsliv are also reserved in their comments and say they will return when there is something to report.

## Disagreement over the costs

According to Arbetet, the parties are fundamentally positive to more people who work being covered by pension contributions and insurance cover. The disagreement is mainly about how the costs are to be shared and how fast the new rules are to be introduced.

LO has earlier criticised that pension contributions and transition cover stop at age 65, even though more and more people are expected to work longer.

## Risk of a break in pension contributions and insurance cover

The current agreements expire on 30 November. If the parties do not manage to agree before then, a state without an agreement could arise from 1 December.

That could mean that contributions to the occupational pension stop and that employees are temporarily without certain insurances under collective agreements, for example cover for work injury.

A state without an agreement also means that the peace obligation ends. Both unions and employers can then take industrial action. LO's leadership has already opened the possibility of giving strike notice.

## What the unions want to change

Two important issues in the negotiations are:

- **Occupational pension:** The unions want to abolish the age limits for pension contributions. Today, accrual for privately employed workers stops at age 65.
- **Transition insurance (AGB):** The unions want to discuss the compensation from the insurance, which also ends at age 65.

The outcome of the talks could therefore be of great importance for two million employees' occupational pensions and insurance cover.
