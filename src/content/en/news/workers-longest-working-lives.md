Machine operators, printers and smiths are among those who worked the longest before retiring. A new survey shows that manual workers dominate the occupations with the most working years, while lawyers are at the bottom. But the statistics need to be read with some care.

The Swedish trade union paper Arbetet had the Swedish Pensions Agency (Pensionsmyndigheten) compile the average number of working years for different occupational groups. The result shows that 26 of the 30 occupations with the longest working lives are manual jobs.

## The occupations with the longest working lives

At the top are machine operators in the rubber, plastic and paper products industry, with an average of 46.4 working years. They are followed by prepress technicians, printers and bookbinders, and by smiths and toolmakers, both with 46.2 years.

The top three, most working years:

- Machine operators in the rubber, plastic and paper products industry: 46.4 years
- Prepress technicians, printers, bookbinders and others: 46.2 years
- Smiths, toolmakers and others: 46.2 years

At the other end of the list are lawyers with 42.1 years, followed by event sellers, telephone sellers and others with 42.2 years. Mixed crop and livestock farmers have an average of 42.4 years.

The average for all occupational groups in the survey is 43.8 years.

## The statistics have important limits

The figures apply to people born in Sweden who started to draw their pension in 2024. The occupation is the one the person had when the pension started.

The measure covers years with pensionable income. That means summer jobs and extra jobs during studies can also be counted. Periods with, for example, sickness benefit and unemployment benefit can be included too.

So the statistics do not show how many years a person actually worked full time or earned a living from work. A person who studied for several years but worked during the summers can, for example, have those years included in the measure.

The selection is also limited to people born in Sweden, because some occupational groups have a high share of foreign-born workers. When the Pensions Agency calculates working years for a broader population, the average is lower.

The survey therefore gives a picture of how long people had pensionable income before retiring, but not a complete measure of the actual time in work.
